Audio gives the timeline its pace. Decide what each layer is responsible for before adding more sound.

| Layer | Main job |
| --- | --- |
| Voiceover | Carry the explanation or story |
| Source audio | Preserve speech or sound from existing footage |
| Music | Set pace, tone, and structure |
| Sound effects | Reinforce a specific action or transition |

## Create voiceover

Give the agent the approved script and voice direction:

```text
Generate the approved SCRIPT.md as a warm, direct voiceover.
Natural pace, no announcer energy. Keep the product name pronunciation exact.
```

The project may keep the spoken text, audio file, and word-level transcript separately so the voice can be regenerated without losing timing work.

## Mix for understanding

- Voice should remain clear over music.
- Use sound effects for meaningful events, not every movement.
- Duck music under important speech.
- Avoid cutting words or breaths accidentally at scene boundaries.
- Listen on ordinary headphones and laptop speakers.

## Keep timing connected

When narration drives the project, use its real transcript timing rather than estimated scene lengths.

For speech captions, continue to [Transcribe and create captions](/guides/transcribe-and-caption).
